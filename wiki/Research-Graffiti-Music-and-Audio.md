## JetSetCraft: the 100-track music discovery atlas

**100 specific recordings and tracks · 66 distinct artist/collaboration credits · 12 clickable cover cards · 6 official video destinations · checked October 31, 2026**

The strongest direction is a city with several musical lives: unmistakable Jet Set funk at its centre, distinct crew identities, a fast jungle and breakbeat lane, and enough warmth for the hours between battles. **Start with the 16-track listening path below.** Then use the seven shelves to find the right sound for a scene instead of treating the full list as one enormous shuffle queue.

These are **new audition recommendations**. All are uncontacted; permission has not been requested. The owner’s existing selections and statuses stay in their canonical wiki registers. No recordings have been downloaded or bundled. Artist/label metadata and links were checked; listening, beat analysis and in-game testing are still to do. The scene placements and taste notes below are original audition proposals, not claims of measured BPM, key, stems, exact drops, or successful integration.

### What is genuinely new in this pass

The **2026 shelf** includes 2 Mello’s *rhythm pools 02 — effervesce* (August 7), GRRL’s *Beetle* (March 13), wev’s *OST* (May 29), and seven individually credited tracks from the *Denshattack!* soundtrack (July 15). The *Denshattack!* connection is especially productive because [Trick Trains and Create](https://github.com/Herbertofury/JetSetCraft/wiki/Trick-Trains-and-Create) is already an approved direction in the live wiki. These soundtrack tracks are additional audition proposals, not newly approved music selections.

Bteck’s [*TECKTONIKS*](https://bteck.bandcamp.com/album/tecktoniks) is dated **October 26, 2026**, making it a particularly fresh Krumpaz discovery. Reso’s [*Reflections EP*](https://reso.bandcamp.com/album/reflections-ep) is a **2026 release of archival 2007–2008 material**, and DJ Paypal’s *Sold Out* remains a **2015 release** even though its current label page describes later artwork. Those distinctions matter when selecting the exact recording.

### Make the soundtrack participate in the game

The repository already documents **80 original 3.2-second gang entrance stingers**, stable gang IDs and data-driven music references. Those are concrete foundations. Full-track music integration, synchronized dance playback and provider behaviour should remain labelled implementation work until demonstrated. See the [boombox runtime contract](https://github.com/Herbertofury/JetSetCraft/blob/main/docs/BOOMBOX_GANG_RUNTIME.md) and the [live music/dance specification](https://github.com/Herbertofury/JetSetCraft/wiki/Music‐Driven-Dances-and-Mob‐Themed-Bosses).

#### 1. Give each district a small musical vocabulary

Choose a handful of compatible **roles**, not a random mountain of songs: ordinary exploration, heightened pursuit, social gathering, crew challenge, victory and cooldown. A neighborhood should be recognizable before a title card appears. Use the atlas to audition alternatives for those roles; do not force every crew into the same sound because its songs share a rough tempo.

A useful first prototype has one warm home district, one fast technical route and one dramatic rival encounter. That gives the music a chance to express contrast. Expanding to every crew before these three scenes work would make a huge library harder to judge.

#### 2. Preserve immediate player control and sound feedback

The owner’s Killer Instinct-level ambition is about music responding to combat and trick chains. Landings, jumps, hits, inputs and simulation must remain immediate. Short original accents can acknowledge actions immediately; longer arrangement changes can enter at an appropriate musical boundary. Do not delay a successful jump until the next beat or silently change physics to fit a song.

If an authorized recording arrives as one stereo file, treat it as one stereo file. Layering clean, original reaction sounds over it is a more credible first prototype than promising nonexistent stems. Where actual editable stems or alternate arrangements become available, use them to make a clearly audible difference between cruising, a growing combo and a major payoff.

#### 3. Keep one event timeline and one clear musical owner

A boombox event, a rival challenge and a Honkistas takeover need an explicit handoff. The preceding event ends; the rival owns its challenge cue; the next takeover receives ownership only at the appropriate event transition. Avoid overlapping full songs, duplicate late-join starts, or a victorious stinger accidentally retriggering the entire encounter.

Late-arriving players need the current event, cue identity and meaningful playback position. Where a source cannot seek reliably, use an agreed join point or an original fallback rather than pretending all clients are synchronized. Transitions should have defined cancellation and cleanup behaviour, including travel, disconnects and the event ending before a track does.

#### 4. Give the Honkistas takeover a recognizable musical idea

The strongest proposal is **recognition followed by transformation**. An original motif heard in the preceding encounter returns in a surprising clown-associated arrangement, then gives way to a distinct challenge score. The player understands what has been hijacked. The Clown Core and Igorrr links are useful studies in contrast; they are not a prescription to paste chaotic full songs over existing music.

A sudden edit should still preserve fair hazard cues and readable animation. Visual choreography, music, announcer payoff and temporary lighting should refer to the same event progression. A surprise can be funny and threatening without making the game hard to parse.

#### 5. Keep music packs convenient and credits visible

Extend the current stable identities with optional, explicit music-pack definitions. A reviewable record would identify the exact track/version, local asset or supported provider, public credit link, any validated cue markers, and a known fallback. “Original Mix,” “Live,” “Extended Version,” “Instrumental,” and “Remix” are meaningful distinctions, not decorative suffixes.

Keep external audio loading out of world startup and keep a clear now-playing credit near the boombox/music interface. If a pack is missing, the original stinger and base event should still make sense. Loudness should be evaluated in the actual game mix, with music, announcer, crew voice and skating sound independently controllable. Muted play must still communicate the event and its payoff visually.

#### 6. Ask for useful deliverables when a track wins the audition

The next useful artist conversation is concrete: the exact recording, the scene it supports, whether a full track or a bespoke cue is wanted, and whether stems, instrumental, clean edit, loopable sections or a short victory ending actually exist. The public artist/label entry URL is retained for every candidate in the JSON; no contacts have been guessed and nobody has been messaged.

This also makes a commission brief much better: “a recognizable crew hook, an exploration arrangement, an escalation arrangement and a short original payoff” gives an artist a game design problem to solve. Avoid turning the reference into an instruction to copy another artist’s melody or voice.

### The audition that would settle the first soundtrack choices

Use the same three short gameplay captures for each of the 16 starting candidates: a free-skating line, an encounter with a readable combo escalation, and a quiet social space. Judge each recording on four practical questions: does it give the scene an identity; does it help the player feel the movement; can important sound effects and dialogue still be understood; and does the cue remain enjoyable on repeated attempts?

Record an exact recording/version, the intended role, any actual timestamps discovered during listening, and the reason a candidate wins or loses. Leave tempo, key, phrase markers and usable loop points blank until they have been checked. That produces a small, defensible first selection while keeping the other discoveries ready for future districts and crews.
