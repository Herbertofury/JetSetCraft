# Footworkas

## Approved identity and design status

**Footworkas** is the approved name of JetSetCraft's Chicago footwork rival gang. Together with Krumpaz, it expands the rival lineup to six: Gabberina, Cumbias, Phonkistas, Hardtekkas, Krumpaz and Footworkas. Existing factions retain their identities and content. The name and dance direction are approved; the gameplay treatments below are proposals and implementation requirements, not shipped features. Final members, genders, boss names and unlock conditions remain undecided.

Footworkas should make close battles feel like rhythmic conversations: intricate lower-body phrases, abrupt changes of emphasis, confident pauses and precise answers. Their competitive identity comes from musical invention and control.

## Cultural and musical foundation

Chicago footwork is a specific Black dance and music culture with a strong battle tradition. [The Era's own account](https://www.theerafootworkcollective.com/about-chicago-footwork) describes its combination-based development, foundations such as Erk N Jerkz and the familiar 160 BPM relationship between music and movement. Connected juke traditions deserve their own context; Chicago footwork should also remain distinct from Detroit jit and generic fast stepping.

Develop choreography with credited practitioners and preserve individual expression. The proposed musical treatment explores rhythmic subdivisions, surprising accent placement and interlocking patterns that leave space for dancers to answer. The genre reference is a foundation, not a universal tempo requirement for every encounter. Two creative track references are now owner-approved below; the remaining recommendations still await selection. Research references provide no recording, choreography, remix or distribution rights.

## Movement on foot and on skates

Create separate on-foot and skating vocabularies. On foot, research authentic combinations and weight changes with practitioners. On skates, translate rhythmic intent into short crossovers, controlled lateral rolls, alternating edges, pivots and resolved glides. Model balance, traction, momentum and plausible wheel contact. Grounded sneaker footplants cannot simply gain wheels and remain mechanically credible.

Proposed skating exchanges can repeat a compact route with a different accent pattern, answer a rival's lateral phrase with a pivot, or finish a rapid sequence in a clean sustained glide. These are original adaptations, not claims about authentic Chicago footwork technique. Distinctive timing must remain visible on the actual character rig and at gameplay distance; animation speed alone cannot supply complexity.

## Shared dance and reactive rave systems

Use the common music-driven generation system to compose varied phrases from compatible authored movements and permitted beat, phrase and section cues. Constrain selection by rig, speed, terrain and current action. Preserve recurring motifs and meaningful answers so generated variation remains musically legible.

Support players and enemies across solo rounds, head-to-head exchanges, crew relays, counterpoint and coordinated endings. A crew can pass a motif between members before reassembling into a shared phrase. Keep player participation voluntary. Competitive scoring should reward the intended movement and phrasing without making rapid repetitive button presses the sole path to success.

Apply the shared Killer Instinct-inspired payoff requirement to both sides. Confirmed trick chains, landings, major payoffs, breaks and recovery should coordinate original music accents, lights, lasers and crew reactions. Proposed Footworkas presentation uses traveling light patterns to reveal rhythmic pathways, then brings the surrounding crew and wider arena into a decisive phrase ending. Nighttime events pursue a complete music-synchronized rave experience. Preserve opponents, terrain, hazards and score cues under peak spectacle; do not delay input or physics for musical alignment. Exact reactivity needs authorized game-native audio and cues, with honest fallbacks for unsupported external providers.

## Rival influence and progression

Footworkas belongs to the shared rival influence, territory, reputation and takeover systems. Proposed surprises include a familiar movement motif returning with shifted emphasis, a different crew member answering, or a readable route variation. Anticipation and fair counterplay must survive the surprise. Each rival faction can be defeated independently, with its own persistent outcome rather than a global reset.

Preserve the shared full-takeover rule: borrowed mob abilities are stripped. Cached state, save/reload, respawns and style customization must not restore stripped powers, revive defeated rivals or duplicate rewards. Preserve source mob identity and unrelated state.

Reputation can support music and dance customization under the shared ownership and permission model. Ranks, thresholds, affected scope and unlock conditions remain design decisions. Personal choices must not silently overwrite another player's shared event. Bosses remain human with mob-themed silhouettes, choreography and borrowed-ability presentation. Their final motifs, roster and names remain open.

## Courses and validation

Use temporary, terrain-aware event circuits with readable battle spaces, crossing routes and clear exits. Preserve vanilla terrain and player builds; remove event-owned effects and actors through the full event lifecycle. New underwater skating requires deliberate three-dimensional movement, depth cues, readable opponents, safe exits and tested surface transitions. It needs its own compatible choreography rather than ground stepping played underwater.

Native acceptance tests must cover:

- Foot contact, momentum, rapid phrase transitions, varied rigs and underwater control.
- Player/enemy solos, head-to-head fairness, crew formation continuity and concurrent combo payoffs.
- Independent defeat, takeover power stripping, reputation permissions and save/reload persistence.
- Reduced-flash/no-strobe, reduced motion, adjustable intensity and usable muted-play cues without scoring penalties.
- Multiplayer lag, late joins, missing assets, offline fallback and complete cleanup.
- Frame/tick stability, memory and startup comparisons, preserving features and participation without arbitrary caps or regressions.
